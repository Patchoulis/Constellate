## 1. The five tools

### 1.1 QED Science ("q.e.d", "QED")

**Which product this is.** "QED" names several unrelated things (proof assistants, consultancies, small GitHub projects). The one meant here is **QED Science (qedscience.com, written "q.e.d")**, founded by Oded Rechavi (Tel Aviv University) and colleagues. It is the only "QED" that is an AI tool for _evaluating and critiquing_ scientific papers and claims. It is also the one that shows up next to Consensus, Elicit, scite and SciSpace in press coverage and "AI tools for research" lists, and bioRxiv/medRxiv chose it as their first automated review pilot (Nov 2025). Its home field is **life sciences**: the benchmark corpus, The 1% ranking and the Insights Feed all draw on bioRxiv and life-science journals. Nothing suggests it is tuned for ML conference papers.

**What it is.** QED Science breaks a manuscript into its claims, arranges them in a dependency tree, and for each claim lists "gaps" where the authors' data or the cited literature do not support it, with suggested fixes. It also scores papers for originality and validity (the "QED Score") and runs a personalised feed of new papers. It is free for academics; the company plans to earn its money from pharma and biotech.

### 1.4 scite (scite.ai)

**What it is.** scite is a citation index that stores, for each citation, the citing sentence with its neighbours (a "citation statement") and labels it **supporting, contrasting or mentioning** with a deep-learning classifier. On top of this index it offers search over citation statements, per-paper reports, a reference checker for manuscripts, collections with alerts, and an AI Assistant answering from full text.

### 1.5 SciSpace (scispace.com, formerly Typeset)

## 3. What Constellate should consider, ranked

Ranked by value to someone mapping a field from 5,000–15,000 papers, weighed against cost and fit with the principles: local except LLM calls and fetching papers, no third-party research APIs, estimates labelled, every model claim cites papers.

A note on cost that applies to every LLM item: any feature that calls the model **once per paper across a whole run** (10k papers × full text) is expensive enough that it must be **on demand, per community, with a cost estimate shown before running** (Elicit shows a credit estimate per column before you turn on high accuracy). Features that call the model **once per community** (about 50–300 calls per run) are cheap enough to run by default. Use the API's batch and prompt-caching options for per-paper jobs, and cache every result by paper ID plus prompt version so that nothing is paid for twice.

### 1. Quote-grounded citations in everything the model writes (Consensus Citation Grounding, Elicit cell quotes)

- **In Constellate.** Every `[paper]` citation in a community overview (and in every later feature below) carries the **exact sentence** from the abstract or PDF that supports it. Hovering shows the sentence and section; clicking opens the reader scrolled to and highlighted at that passage. A small marker says whether the quote came from the abstract or the full text.
- **Already has.** Overviews that cite papers; abstracts; PDF text; the reader.
- **To build.** Ask the model to return `{paper_id, quote}` pairs, then **verify each quote locally** by fuzzy string match against the stored text (Rust). Reject or flag citations whose quote is not found. Map PDF text offsets to page coordinates for highlighting.
- **Local?** Yes. Only the existing LLM call; the check is local.
- **Main risk.** Quotes paraphrased by the model fail the match; the prompt should allow retry or downgrade to "cited, quote not verified". PDF text extraction noise (ligatures, hyphenation) needs normalising before matching.
- **Size.** Small to medium.
- **Learn from.** Consensus: hover shows the sentence plus section header, the PDF opens at the highlight, and a full-text/abstract checkmark. Elicit: "click any cell to see quotes". This is what turns "every claim cites papers" from a promise into something checkable, and every later item reuses it.

### 2. Extraction table across a community, with a supporting quote per cell (Elicit)

- **In Constellate.** A "Table" tab in a community's panel. Rows are the community's papers (or a sub-community, or marked papers); columns are plain-English questions. Defaults suited to ML: _task_, _datasets_, _metrics and headline number_, _baselines compared against_, _model size / compute_, _code released?_, _main claimed contribution_. The user can add columns. Every cell has its quote (item 1) and an "AI-extracted" label. Columns are sortable and filterable, and a column can **colour the graph** (e.g. colour every paper by "dataset = ImageNet").
- **Already has.** Communities, full-text PDFs, the LLM, declarations parsing (which is already a kind of extraction).
- **To build.** Column spec, job runner with per-cell caching, quote verification, table UI, CSV export, colour-by-column on the graph.
- **Local?** Yes, except the LLM calls.
- **Main risk.** Cost: a 400-paper community × one call per paper with all columns over full text is millions of input tokens. Mitigations: start from abstract-only (cheap; Elicit also screens on abstracts first), escalate to full text per column; "pilot on 10 papers" before running on all (Elicit recommends exactly this); show an estimate before running. Accuracy: Elicit's own evaluation (94% by an LLM grader) is a vendor claim; numbers pulled from results tables are the error-prone part.
- **Size.** Medium to large.
- **Learn from.** Elicit: plain-English column names, a pilot run on a subset, quotes in context on click, a "high accuracy" option at higher cost, export that includes the quotes and reasoning. SciSpace: up to 50 columns and export to CSV/BIB/RIS.

### 3. Question answering over a community or the whole run, with cited answers

- **In Constellate.** An "Ask" box at run level and in each community's panel. Answers cite papers with verified quotes (item 1), and the cited papers **light up on the graph**, so the answer is also a map of where the evidence sits.
- **Already has.** Local embeddings (retrieval by question embedding), communities (scoping), the LLM, PDF text.
- **To build.** Local retrieval (embed question; top-k papers by embedding, optionally re-ranked with chunk embeddings of full text), answer prompt, graph highlighting of cited papers, history of questions per run.
- **Local?** Yes. Retrieval is local; one LLM call per question.
- **Main risk.** Retrieval over title+abstract embeddings misses answers that are only in the body. Chunk-level embeddings of full text are needed for good recall, which is substantial local compute for 15k PDFs (fine on GPU; slow on CPU, so make it optional or per community).
- **Size.** Medium.
- **Learn from.** Consensus's trace of what was searched and read, and its warning when only abstracts were read. Elicit asks clarifying questions before long jobs. Do **not** copy the agentic web search; stay inside the run.

### 4. Citation context: citing sentences, then classification (scite)

- **In Constellate.** In the reader's citation section, under "Cited by (in this library)", show for each citing paper **the sentence(s) where it cites this paper** and the section it is in. Then, labelled as an estimate, classify each one. For ML papers the useful labels are richer than scite's three: **uses / builds on**, **compares against (as a baseline)**, **supports / reproduces**, **contrasts / fails to reproduce / criticises**, **mentions**. Community panels can then show "most used as a baseline", "most built upon" and "most contested" papers instead of raw citation counts, and graph edges can be coloured by type.
- **Already has.** Reference lists parsed from PDFs, citation edges within the library, PDF text.
- **To build.** (a) In Rust, find in-text citation markers (`[12]`, `(Smith et al., 2021)`, author-year variants) and link each to its reference entry, then to the library paper. scite does this with GROBID; Constellate would build its own. Matching is easier here because the target set is the known library, not all of Crossref. (b) Take the citing sentence plus the one before and after (scite's window). (c) Classify: either a small local classifier (scite's is a fine-tuned SciBERT; an open model could be fine-tuned and run on the user's GPU, consistent with the local-embedding principle) or LLM calls only for within-library edges.
- **Local?** Snippets: fully local. Classification: local if a small model is shipped; otherwise LLM calls per edge.
- **Main risk.** Accuracy, documented. scite says 92.6% of citations are "mentioning" and only 0.8% contrasting. Its classifier is tuned for precision, and an independent study found it badly under-detects supporting and contrasting citations (F 0.0–0.58). Even scite's PDF pipeline links only about 70% of citation contexts correctly. Volume: a 10k-paper run may have tens of thousands of within-library citation edges, so per-edge LLM calls cost real money; a local model or on-demand classification per paper avoids that.
- **Size.** Snippets: medium. Classification: medium (LLM) to large (local model).
- **Learn from.** scite: always show the snippet, so the label is never the only thing the user sees; show the section; let users correct labels; filter by label and section. Its own paper says the three-way ontology misses nuance and "contrasting" only covers new evidence, which is why richer ML-specific labels fit better.

### 5. Where a community agrees and disagrees (Consensus Meter, adapted)

- **In Constellate.** In a community's panel, the user asks a yes/no-style question ("Does pretraining scale help robustness here?") or picks one the model proposes from the overview's "open questions". Each relevant paper is placed as **Yes / Mixed / No / Not addressed**, with its quote. The result is shown as counts **broken down by acceptance tier and year** and as colours on the graph, so you see whether disagreement is spread out or sits in one sub-community.
- **Already has.** Communities, overviews (which already list open questions), tiers, the LLM.
- **To build.** Question flow, relevance and stance pass per paper (abstract first), aggregation and graph colouring, all labelled as an estimate.
- **Local?** Yes, except the LLM calls.
- **Main risk.** Consensus's own documentation lists the failures: about 10% misclassification, missed nuance (wrong population), **every paper counts equally**, and coverage limited to the top 20. ML results are conditional on datasets and settings, so "yes/no" collapses detail. Mitigations: always show the per-paper quote; never show a single verdict without the distribution; show tier and year next to the counts rather than weighting silently; leave out papers below a relevance/confidence threshold and say how many were left out.
- **Size.** Medium. Cheap once item 2 exists, since a stance is just a column with fixed answers.
- **Learn from.** Consensus's guardrails: extracted rather than generated text, relevance and confidence thresholds, results visible under the meter, a published error rate.

### 6. Claims and reviews mapped together (QED's claim tree + the eLife pilot)

- **In Constellate.** In the reader, a "Claims" view: the paper's main claims as a short tree (claim → sub-claims → the experiments the paper offers for each). Where the app has the OpenReview reviews, **each reviewer comment is attached to the claim it addresses** (what eLife did with QED's tree). Claims no reviewer engaged with, and claims several reviewers questioned, stand out. An optional AI "possible gaps" layer can sit on top, clearly separated and labelled as model-generated.
- **Already has.** The reviews page, declarations, PDF text, the LLM. Nobody else in this survey has the reviews.
- **To build.** Claim extraction prompt (one call per paper, on demand), review-comment segmentation, comment-to-claim assignment, tree UI. At community level: "claims most often challenged by reviewers in this community".
- **Local?** Yes, except the LLM calls.
- **Main risk.** In eLife's pilot about 20% of assignments were judged wrong and some comments were left unassigned, so show unassigned comments rather than hide them. AI-generated weaknesses are the risky part: research on LLM reviews (ReviewScore, ClaimCheck) finds many are misinformed or overstated, so human reviews should be the primary layer and AI gaps secondary and optional. Reviews exist only for OpenReview venues (ICLR, NeurIPS and others); CVPR/ICCV/ECCV and arXiv runs have none.
- **Size.** Medium.
- **Learn from.** QED: claims as the unit of analysis, with gaps attached to claims rather than a flat list of weaknesses; a verification pass that rechecks flagged issues. eLife: use the tree to _organise human reviews_, which is more trustworthy than generating new critique.

### 7. Exports: BibTeX, CSV, RIS and a written report

- **In Constellate.** Export a community, the marked papers or a filtered view as **BibTeX / RIS** (from the official venue metadata the app already has), **CSV** (paper metadata, tier, AI estimate, declarations, extraction-table columns with quotes), and a **Markdown/HTML report** of a run or community (overview, open questions, top papers by citations, tables), with every citation resolved to a bibliography entry. Also export the graph (GraphML or JSON) for people who want to analyse it elsewhere.
- **Already has.** All the data.
- **To build.** Serialisers and a report template.
- **Local?** Fully.
- **Main risk.** Little. BibTeX keys and venue fields need care across conferences.
- **Size.** Small.
- **Learn from.** Elicit's export carries the supporting quotes; SciSpace exports RIS, CSV, BIB, Excel and XML; Consensus exports RIS and CSV for Zotero/EndNote/Mendeley.

### 8. Watch a community for new papers (Elicit Routines, scite collection alerts)

- **In Constellate.** For arXiv-based runs (and new conference lists when they appear), a "watch" setting: periodically fetch new arXiv papers in the run's categories, embed them locally, and **assign each to its nearest community** (k-nearest papers or community centroid) without re-clustering. They show up on the graph with a "new" badge, and the community panel shows a "new since last visit" list. Optionally the model writes a short "what's new in this community" update citing the new papers. That last step is Elicit's key idea: update the artifact, not just send a list.
- **Already has.** arXiv fetching, local embeddings, communities, overviews.
- **To build.** Scheduler (desktop: on launch or a timer; server mode: cron-like), incremental embed and assign, "new" state per paper, notification UI, optional delta overview.
- **Local?** Yes. Fetching arXiv is allowed; assignment is local.
- **Main risk.** Drift: new papers that fit no community well. Show them as "unassigned" and suggest re-clustering when there are many. Delta overviews cost one call per changed community.
- **Size.** Medium.
- **Learn from.** Elicit Routines: daily or weekly, update the existing table or report, report back for review. scite: alerts tied to a saved collection, on by default.

### 9. Gap / evidence matrix for a community (SciSpace Gap Matrix agent, Consensus Deep "gaps")

- **In Constellate.** A grid for a community or the whole run, e.g. **task × method family** or **dataset × approach**, built from extraction columns (item 2). Cell = number of papers, with click-through. Empty or thin cells are candidate gaps. Shown next to the overview's "open questions".
- **Already has.** Communities; item 2 provides the columns.
- **To build.** Normalising values (the model writes "ImageNet-1k", "ImageNet", "IN-1K"; these need merging, either by a single LLM normalisation pass per column or by local embedding clustering of values), grid UI.
- **Local?** Yes.
- **Main risk.** Empty cells are not proof of gaps: a combination may be meaningless, or the papers may sit in a venue outside the run. Consensus's own Skills post warns that absence from a review does not establish a gap. Label empty cells "no paper in this run", not "gap".
- **Size.** Medium (after item 2).
- **Learn from.** SciSpace's gap-matrix framing (strong / thin / missing). Consensus's caveat.

### 10. "Explain this" in the reader (SciSpace Chat with PDF)

- **In Constellate.** Select a passage, equation or table in the reader and choose "Explain". The model explains it, optionally using the paper's own definitions; the answer cites the passages of the paper it used. "Ask this paper" is a natural extension (item 3 scoped to one paper).
- **Already has.** The reader, PDF text, the LLM.
- **To build.** Selection handling in the PDF view. Equations and tables extract poorly as text, so send a **cropped image of the selected region** (Claude accepts images) together with the surrounding text. Answer panel.
- **Local?** Yes, except the call.
- **Main risk.** Low. One call per use; explanations of maths can be wrong, so show the cropped region beside the answer.
- **Size.** Small to medium.
- **Learn from.** SciSpace: highlight → simplified explanation; separate handling for maths, tables and figures; related papers for the highlighted text. Constellate can do the last one **locally** by embedding the highlighted passage and showing its nearest papers in the run, with no model call.

### 11. Place your own paper (or draft) on the map, and check its references

- **In Constellate.** Drop in a PDF (your draft or any paper). Constellate embeds it locally, places it on the graph next to its nearest papers, names the community it falls into, and lists its closest prior work in the run. Optionally: which of its references are in the library and how the community cites them (item 4), and which close papers it does **not** cite.
- **Already has.** Embeddings, nearest neighbours, reference parsing.
- **To build.** Import flow, placement without re-clustering, "close but not cited" list.
- **Local?** Yes.
- **Main risk.** "Not cited but close" is a similarity estimate, not a judgement of missing work. Retraction checks (scite) need outside data Constellate does not have; leave them out.
- **Size.** Small to medium.
- **Learn from.** scite Reference Check and Consensus Verify Citations (check a draft's references); QED's novelty view ("separates novel contributions from existing knowledge"), done here by geometry rather than by an LLM verdict.

### 12. Per-paper AI critique on demand (QED / SciSpace Peer Review agent), kept secondary

- **In Constellate.** A button in the reader: "Possible weaknesses (AI)". It produces claim-anchored concerns (item 6) with quotes, and a check against the paper's own declared limitations ("authors already acknowledge this"). It is never shown by default, never aggregated into a score, and never used to sort.
- **Already has.** Declarations (limitations statements), reviews, LLM.
- **To build.** Prompt with a separate verification pass (QED's white paper describes one), UI.
- **Local?** Yes, except calls.
- **Main risk.** Accuracy and tone: LLM critiques overstate minor issues and repeat things the paper already answers. Where human reviews exist, they are better evidence.
- **Size.** Small (once item 6 exists).
- **Learn from.** QED: specialist checks (statistics, alternative explanations, reporting standards) and a verification layer. Also its controversy: do not compress the critique into one quality number.

### Also worth noting (smaller)

- **Study-snapshot card for ML papers** (Consensus Study Snapshot). A fixed set of extraction columns (task, data, metric, compute, code) shown on every paper card. It is item 2 with fixed columns, run lazily when a paper is opened, and cached. Small once item 2 exists.
- **"Most influential within the community"**. Consensus uses Semantic Scholar's influential-citation model, which is ruled out here; Constellate can compute its own from item 4 (papers cited in methods and results sections, or as baselines, rather than in passing in related work). Small after item 4.
- **Shared, read-only links in server mode** (Elicit view-only links, scite shared collections), once the self-hosted server exists. Medium; not urgent for a single-user tool.

## 5. What could not be verified

- **QED Science**: no public pricing page; no public sample report; the term "claim tree" appears in the eLife article and the VP interview, not on QED's own pages that could be read. The white paper's weights and the models used are not published. The JMIR article's text did not load, so criticism was taken from search summaries of JMIR and The Scientist.
- **Consensus**: the help centre and docs (help.consensus.app, docs.consensus.app) returned 403, so the "Mixed" Meter category, the Consensus Snapshot details and the current state of Ask Paper come from search snippets only. Prices come from an Intercom mirror of the help centre; the live pricing page did not render its plans. Alerts were not found in any primary source.
- **Elicit**: the details of "explanations for AI-generated answers" and of current journal-quality filtering were not opened directly. Some changelog entries are title-only.
- **scite**: help.scite.ai failed (TLS error); Reference Check pricing (about $1 per manuscript, one free a month) is from Nature Index and Aaron Tay [third-party] and may be out of date. The current classifier's per-class numbers (Table 2 of the paper) were not readable.
- **SciSpace**: most scispace.com pages block automated fetching; pages were read in a browser without logging in. The Agent Gallery and data-sources pages render only partly. Premium and Max prices and credit allowances are third-party. Whether extracted cells carry verbatim quotes, as Elicit's do, was not confirmed (the FAQ answer is collapsed). The "Explain math and table" description is from the comparison table and FAQ headings, not a full description.
- **No accuracy numbers here were checked independently**, except scite's classifier (Bakker et al., 2023). Elicit's 94–99% extraction accuracy, QED's AUC and correlation figures, Consensus's 10% error estimate and SciSpace's 98% detector accuracy are the vendors' own.
